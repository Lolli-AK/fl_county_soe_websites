Military & Overseas Citizens Voting

1. Who is Eligible?

The Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA) ensures eligible citizens can apply to register to vote and request a vote-by-mail (absentee ballot). Citizens covered under UOCAVA include:

  • Uniformed Service Members: Active-duty members of the U.S. Armed Forces, Merchant Marine, Commissioned Corps of the Public Health Service, and National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA).
  • Eligible Family Members: Spouses and dependents of uniformed service members.
  • Overseas Citizens: U.S. citizens residing outside the United States, including military personnel stationed abroad and other U.S. citizens living overseas.

Note: Domestic military voters residing in their own voting jurisdictions are not covered under UOCAVA and should vote via standard methods.

2. How to Register & Request a Ballot

You may register to vote and request a Vote-by-Mail ballot using the Federal Post Card Application (FPCA) (PDF) . The FPCA form is made available through the Federal Voting Assistance Program (FVAP) .

  • Form Duration: The FPCA request for a Vote-by-Mail ballot is valid for all elections through the next regularly scheduled general election.
  • Submission: Send a completed FPCA containing your original signature to the Supervisor of Elections.
  • Standard Deadline: It must arrive no later than 29 days before the election.
  • Late Registration Exception: Pursuant to 97.055, F.S. , an individual or accompanying family member who has been discharged or separated from the uniformed services or the U.S. Merchant Marine, or has returned from a military deployment after the book closing date (29 days before an election) and who is otherwise qualified, may register to vote in such election until 5:00 PM on the Friday before that election in the office of the Supervisor of Elections. You must produce sufficient documentation showing evidence of eligibility for late registration.

Alternative Request Methods

You may also request a ballot by phone or written request using the contact information below. Please include your name, address, date of birth, status (military, spouse of military, or overseas), postal address, and email address.

3. Receiving Your Ballot

Ballots for UOCAVA voters are mailed no later than 45 days in advance of the election.

  • By Mail: Your ballot will be mailed to the mailing address on your Vote-by-Mail request form.
  • By Email/Fax: Overseas military, absent stateside military, and overseas civilians may have a Vote-by-Mail ballot emailed or faxed (depending on your request) in lieu of receiving it by regular mail, provided a valid email address is supplied.

5. Critical Deadlines

Stateside Uniformed Voters

  • Receipt: To be accepted and counted, a voted Vote-by-Mail ballot from an absent stateside uniformed services voter must be received by the Supervisor of Elections no later than 7:00 PM on Election Day in the time zone for the county in which the voter is registered.

Overseas Voters

  • Extension: Overseas voters casting ballots in a Presidential Preference Primary or General Election are allowed a 10-day extension.
  • Postmark: The ballot must be postmarked or dated no later than Election Day .
  • Receipt: It must be received by the Supervisor of Elections of the county in which the voter is registered no later than 10 days after Election Day .

6. Provisional Ballots

(See sections 101.048 and 101.043 , Florida Statutes, and Rule 1S-2.037 , Florida Administrative Code)

If your eligibility to vote cannot be determined, you will be allowed to vote a provisional ballot. A person casting a provisional ballot may present written evidence supporting his or her eligibility to vote to the Supervisor of Elections no later than 5:00 PM on the second day following the election .
