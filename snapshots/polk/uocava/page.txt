Military & Overseas Voting
U.O.C.A.V.A
The Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act or UOCAVA pertains to voters who are non-military U.S. citizens residing outside the United States, or who are active members of the Uniformed Services, the Merchant Marines, and the commissioned corps of the Public Health Service and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and their eligible family members who reside outside the United States. This Act provides the legal basis for these citizens' ability to exercise their right to vote.
If you are overseas military or a citizen and would like to vote, are not yet registered to vote or would like to submit a request for a mail ballot from overseas, you can submit a Federal Post Card Application (FPCA) to your local Supervisor of Elections office.
F.P.C.A
An overseas citizen of the United States can register to vote and request an absentee ballot by submitting
the Federal Postcard Application (FPCA)
to the elections office. Once registered to vote, members of the uniformed services and their families as well as eligible voters staying overseas and their families are eligible to have a mail ballot sent to them by mail, fax or email.
Emailed Ballots
The voter is sent a ballot containing a link and a passcode. After marking the ballot online, the voter must print the ballot and voter's certificate and return the voted ballot and signed voter's certificate. Overseas voters are permitted to use any of the return methods available to stateside voters, but only they are allowed to also return the voted ballot and voter's certificate by fax. Detailed instructions are included with the ballot when it's delivered by email.
Ballot Not Received
Please note: mail ballots (including ballots delivered by email or fax) begin being sent to active-duty members of the uniformed services and overseas voters and their spouses around 45 days prior to an election, after which time they will be send within two days of a new request received by the Supervisor of Elections.
If you have not received your mail ballot, you can:
Confirm
your request
and delivery method: please contact our office and confirm we have a mail ballot request on file for you and that your fax number, email or mailing address is accurate. You can also
check the status online
.
Request
another mail ballot
: the deadline for the Supervisor of Elections to receive a request for a mail ballot to be mailed is twelve days prior to Election Day at 5 p.m. After that deadline, persons eligible to have mail ballots delivered to them by email or fax. Requests received after 7 p.m. on Election Day will not be processed for that election.
Request an emailed or faxed ballot
: If the deadline for the Supervisor of Elections to receive a request for a ballot to be mailed has passed, then be aware that active-duty uniformed services and overseas voters may still
request a ballot to be emailed or faxed.
If you do not receive your emailed or faxed ballot within two business days, please contact our office.
Request a Backup Ballot
: If you have made a timely request for but have not received a vote-by-mail ballot, then you may use the Federal Write-in Absentee Ballot (FWAB).
F.W.A.B.
The Federal-Write-in Absentee Ballot, or FWAB, is a backup ballot that may only be used by active-duty uniformed services and overseas voters. A voter who submits the FWAB and later receives the official mail ballot may still submit the official mail ballot. The deadline for the Supervisor of Elections to receive a FWAB is the same as if the FWAB were an official ballot. If the FWAB is received by the deadline, it will be canvassed. However, if the voted official mail ballot is received by the Supervisor of Elections by 7 p.m. on Election Day, or, in a presidential preference primary or general election, no later than 10 days after the date of the election, the FWAB may not be valid and will be referred to the canvassing board for review.
Obtaining & Completing the FWAB
Download the
FWAB request form (PDF)
or view
form details on the Federal Voting Assistance Program website
. The FWAB download contains instructions, a page for you to enter your complete voter information with a handwritten signature, and a page for you to write in your voting choices.
You can find the races you are eligible to vote in, as well as the candidates running, by pulling up your voter status.
FWAB Return
Overseas voters may return the FWAB by mail (using sufficient postage or by using the postage-paid template included in the FWAB instructions), by having the FWAB delivered to the office of the supervisor of elections. Voters eligible to use the FWAB can send the voted FWAB and completed Voter Information page to:
By mail to: Supervisor of Elections, P.O. Box 7, Auburndale FL 33823
By fax to
(863) 845-2719
FWAB Return Deadlines
The FWAB must be received by the Supervisor of Elections by 7 p.m. Eastern Time on Election Day. However, overseas voters have a 10-day deadline extension for ballots returned by mail in a presidential preference primary election and in a general election. For an overseas ballot to count when received by the Supervisor of Elections after 7 p.m. on Election Day in either a presidential preference primary election or a general election, the overseas voted ballot must be postmarked or dated no later than Election Day and received by the Supervisor of Elections no later than 10 days after Election Day.
For more information about voting overseas, contact the Vote-by-Mail Department
:
