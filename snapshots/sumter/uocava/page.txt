Military & Overseas Voters OR Residency Questions
What is UOCAVA?
The Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act is commonly referred to as UOCAVA. UOCAVA citizens are U.S. citizens who are active members of the Uniformed Services, the Merchant Marine, and the commissioned corps of the Public Health Service and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, their eligible family members, and U.S. citizens residing outside the United States. This act provides the legal basis for these citizens' absentee voting requirements for federal offices.

Questioning your Residency?
Voter Residency Guidelines in Florida
- Use this link
NOTE: Number 7 within the Residency Guidelines refers to being a U.S. citizen born OR living abroad. It states that a U.S. citizen, who is born or living abroad and has never lived in Florida, is not permitted to register or vote in Florida under current Florida law.
In order to qualify as a Registered Voter in Florida, to include Sumter County, while living overseas, number 7 in the Residency Guidelines has to be met prior to meeting numbers Guidelines 1 or 2.

Ballot Delivery
If you are an overseas voter (this does not include U.S. territories) or an absent uniformed services voter with an applicable out-of-county address, you may request to receive your ballots via fax or email. Ballots are not forwardable and will be mailed no later than 45 days before each Election.
Ballot Return

Federal Write-In Absentee Ballot (FWAB)
The FWAB (Federal Standard Form 186) serves as an emergency backup ballot for UOCAVA citizens. If you do not receive your absentee ballot from your state in time to return it to your election official to participate in the election, use the FWAB.
