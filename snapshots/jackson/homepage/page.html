Primary Elections

Primary elections play a crucial role in shaping the political landscape by determining which candidates will appear on the general election ballot. As a closed primary state, Florida restricts participation in partisan primaries to those voters who are registered members of their respective political parties. This system ensures that party nominees reflect the preferences of their registered members. In non-partisan races, such as those for school board members and county judges, those are typically decided during primary elections allowing all registered voters to participate regardless of party affiliation. Consequently, primary elections often serve as the decisive contest for these positions emphasizing the importance of voter participation in the primaries to influence local governance and policy decisions.

Universal Primary

There are times when all registered voters can vote in a primary election, regardless of which political party they are registered with, or even if they are registered with no party affiliation. During these elections, the race is considered a Universal Primary Contest. This occurs if all the candidates for an office have the same party affiliation, and the winner of the primary election will not face any opposition in the general election (i.e., no write-in candidates have qualified). All registered voters can vote for any of the candidates for that office in the primary election. And the winner of the primary is declared the winner for that particular race.

Party Affiliation

Party affiliation means being officially connected to a political party such as the Democratic Party, Republican Party or a recognized minor political party. When you register to vote you can choose to affiliate with a party or register with no party at all. Your party affiliation will affect which candidates you can vote for in primary elections as Florida is a closed primary state. Choosing a party helps you signal your general political beliefs and values. Also, being affiliated with a party can open opportunities to participate more deeply in politics by attending gatherings and meetings with your chosen party. You can change your party affiliation in the future should your values or ideals change.

Early Voting

Early voting is a process that allows registered voters to cast their ballots before Election Day. It was designed to make voting more accessible and convenient, especially for voters who have work conflicts, are travelling out of town on Election Day or have other obstacles on Election Day. Early Voting hours are 8- 5:30 p.m. daily. There are typically 10- 12 days of early voting leading up to Election Day. If you choose to vote during Early Voting hours, you can experience shorter lines and less crowding at the polls, have the ability to resolve any registration issues, and enjoy more flexibility if you have work and family obligations.

Book Closing / Registration Deadline

Florida election law requires a deadline for all voter registrations of twenty-nine (29) days before an election. Registration books close on the 29th day before any election and remain closed until after that election. Updates to a voter’s name, address, or signature are the only changes that can be made to a voter record while the books are closed that take effect immediately. If a change of party affiliation is made, that will take effect in subsequent elections.

Message from Carol A. Dunaway, Supervisor of Elections

Thank you for visiting our website and thank you for your interest in the election process and your desire to be a more informed voter. The right to vote is one of the most important privileges we enjoy as citizens of the United States. Along with this privilege comes great responsibility to not only become informed, but to cast that all important vote, your vote, during the election process.
